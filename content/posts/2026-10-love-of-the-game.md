I always liked building things.

I built mazes (from decommissioned [punched tape]). Then LEGOs, and [robots] made of LEGOs. \
Then I built software for the robots, and then... just pure software.

[punched tape]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punched_tape
[robots]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego_Mindstorms#RCX

I had always built these things **because it was fun to build them** and because the result itself was fun to play with.

Then, software became a vocation.
Building good software, even just working software, ~was~ is *hard*.

Didn't add a test *before* writing a fix? The bug will come back. Flaky CI? Users will complain that releases are broken. New index but forgot to do it CONCURRENTLY? There goes your weekend.

So you _had_ to care and get good at building, which ~was~ is rewarding in itself: learning, trying, succeeding and failing, teaching others.

There's a [long] and [storied] [tradition] of [FAFO-ing] things in software.
You just _had_ to put in the effort. You _had_ to care. There were (almost) no shortcuts.

[long]: https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~EWD/transcriptions/EWD10xx/EWD1036.html
[storied]: https://www.usenix.org/system/files/1311_05-08_mickens.pdf
[tradition]: https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2000/04/06/things-you-should-never-do-part-i/
[FAFO-ing]: https://github.com/danluu/post-mortems


But recently there's been a lot more Fucking Around and significantly less Finding Out, at least in the short-to-mid term.
I hope some people will still care about the craft, but it seems likely they won't _have to_, and maybe it's time to accept that.

So while the future plays out, I'm going to keep building. \
You don't _have_ to care anymore. But I still do.

I'm in it for the love of the game. Always have been.
